# Quick concept games: how they teach

Written 2026-10-27 for 1.25. Code: `assets/js/concepts.js`. Evidence: the [research folder](../research/INDEX.md) (7 files, ~200 sources).

## What it is

While he plays the piano, after some minutes of real playing (default 5), at the first pause of 1.5 s in his notes, a **Quick game** opens: 2 questions (1–3), then "Great! Back to the piano!" Now and then the same games appear at a break in Colouring (after a finished picture) and in Bigger or smaller (between questions). Each game teaches **basic relational concepts**: the words school and everyday language assume he knows.

## The concepts (teaching order)

| # | Pair | Shown as | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | bigger / smaller | the same thing at two sizes | the general size word comes first (research/01) |
| 2 | louder / quieter | the same note played hard and gently | piano-native, KS1 music (06) |
| 3 | longer / shorter | pencils or ropes from one start line | Y1 maths measures (07) |
| 4 | higher / lower (notes) | two notes an octave+ apart; "high notes live on the right" | piano-native; teach the word with the keys (06) |
| 5 | taller / shorter | towers of blocks on one ground line | after "big" (01) |
| 6 | full / empty | identical glasses | identical glasses avoid the tall-thin trap (04) |
| 7 | heavier / lighter | a see-saw, both things drawn the same size | the tilt is the cue, not the size (04) |
| 8 | closer to / further from the house | the same animal near or far from a house | a landmark, not depth cues (04) |
| 9 | above / below the shelf | a toy over or under a shelf | Y1 position words (07) |
| 10 | inside / outside the box | a toy in or out of a box | EYFS/Y1 position |
| 11 | in front of / behind the box | a ball in front, or hidden behind | occlusion; still developing at 6–7 (01) |
| 12 | between | three animals in a row, tap the middle one | still hard at 6–7 (01) |
| 13 | first / last | four animals walking to a flag | the direction of travel is shown (04) |
| 14 | faster / slower | the same vehicle on the same track | one dimension (04) |
| 15 | longer / shorter (notes) | the same note held or cut | KS1 duration (06) |
| 16 | thicker / thinner | two bars, same height | Y1 vocabulary |
| 17 | wider / narrower | two doors, same height | Y1 vocabulary |
| 18 | hot / cold | objects (steam, fire, ice, snow), never red/blue tints | colour does not mean temperature at 6 (04) |
| 19 | darker / lighter colour | one shape, one hue, two lightness levels | colour-blind safe; Y3 enrichment, so late (04, 07) |
| 20 | brighter / dimmer | a lamp shining or not | Y3 light, enrichment |

Left/right is deliberately not included yet: on his own body it is secure around 7, on a picture or person facing him around 8–9 (01, 04).

## How a concept is taught

1. **One word first.** "bigger" only, until 2 right in a row (research/01: one pole first, "big / not big").
2. **The first time a word comes it is shown, not asked.** The right picture glows, the voice says "Look! This one is bigger. It takes up more space. Tap it!" (errorless start, 01/03).
3. **Then the other word** ("smaller"), shown first, then asked until 2 in a row.
4. **Then both mixed**, until 3 in a row **and** on at least two different days (spacing, 03). A concept that passed for today rests until tomorrow, so new ones keep coming.
5. **Learned concepts come back** after 1, 2, 4, 7 and 14 days; a miss steps back one gap (03).
6. **Every question is a new picture**: other animals, toys, colours, sizes (multiple exemplars, 01/03).
7. **Two concepts are in play at once** and a game never asks the same concept twice in a row, so it never feels like the same game (his short focus window; 02).

## Focus, the way the research says

- Short: 2 questions, about 20–40 s, only at a natural pause in his playing (02).
- Visible: "Quick game! 2 questions, then back to your song" and two dots (02).
- Movement break every 4th game: "Reach your hands up high!", "Touch the floor, down low!", "Clap in front of you!", "Hands behind your back!" — movement breaks raise on-task behaviour most in the least focused children (Mahar 2006 and later trials, 02), and they act out the space words (01).
- At most 4 games an hour, at least 5 minutes apart (02).
- Two misses in one game: it ends kindly and he goes back to playing (fatigue sign, 02/03).
- Feedback says what is true: "Yes! That one is heavier." A miss shows the right one with one reason, starting with his name: "Hey Sam, not quite. This one is heavier. The heavy side goes down." (03, 05)
- A ⚡ appears for a fast right answer (under 3 s). Nothing is ever taken away; no timers (02, 05). The research warns that expected rewards can lower children's own interest (03), so stars stay small and informational.
- We do not claim the games "train attention": brain-training transfer is weak (02, 06). They teach concepts; the piano is the reward.

## Grown-ups' Menu → Maths → Quick concept games

- A quick game every: 3 / 5 / 8 / 10 min of piano, or off
- Questions each time: 1 / 2 / 3
- Also in colouring and Bigger or smaller: yes / no
- Move breaks: yes / no
- Try one now (shows how many are learned and which two he is learning)
- Start the concept games again
