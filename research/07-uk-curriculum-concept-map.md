# 07 — UK curriculum concept map: every basic concept by the end of Year 2
Last updated: 2026-10-27 · Researcher: subagent · Keywords: EYFS 2026, early learning goals, Development Matters, national curriculum, key stage 1, Year 1, Year 2, mathematics programme of study, measurement vocabulary, comparatives, longer shorter, heavier lighter, full empty, half full, quicker slower, earlier later, position and direction, left right, above between, turns, clockwise, 2D 3D shapes, fractions, ready-to-progress criteria, NCETM, Mastering Number, science materials, hard soft, shiny dull, transparent opaque, seasons, light and dark Year 3, music dimensions, pitch, dynamics, tempo, Model Music Curriculum, art elements, colour, texture, tone, English suffixes -er -est, conjunctions, Curriculum and Assessment Review 2028, concept pairs, mini-games

## Answer in 5 lines
By the end of Year 2 a child in England is expected to have the comparison language of size, length/height, weight, capacity, temperature, time, money, number, position, turning and shape, plus the Year 1 science property pairs (hard/soft, rough/smooth, shiny/dull, see-through/not…) and the music pairs high/low, loud/quiet, fast/slow, long/short.
Almost all of the "named" vocabulary lists are in the **non-statutory** notes of the Year 1 maths and science programmes; the statutory lines just say "compare, describe and solve practical problems" — so the app should treat those lists as the checklist, not as optional extras.
"Light vs dark" (as in seeing) is statutory only in Year 3 science, and "tone" (lighter/darker colours) is not in the KS1 art list at all — still worth covering, but labelled as stretch/enrichment.
The master table below has 127 concept pairs, each tagged with curriculum year and a tablet representation; nearly all can be shown as picture + spoken word, and the music ones are native to the piano screen.
Teach in 11 short units, starting with what EYFS should already have secured (big/small, more/fewer, position) and the piano-native sound pairs, then the Year 1 comparatives, then Year 2 three-way ordering (-est, <, >, =), with Year 3 light/dark last.

## Findings

### F1. The current EYFS framework (from 1 Sept 2026) keeps the same goals for comparing and shape/space/measure  `evidence: guideline (statutory)`
The DfE published new EYFS statutory frameworks (group/school-based and childminder versions) in July 2026, in force from 1 September 2026 [S2][S3]. The end-of-Reception goals read in the 2026 text: **ELG Number** — deep understanding of numbers to 10 and how each is made up, subitising (recognising without counting) up to 5, and automatic recall of bonds to 5 and some to 10 including doubles. **ELG Numerical Patterns** — count aloud past 20, compare quantities up to 10 and recognise when one is "greater than, less than or the same as" another, and explore evens/odds, doubles and fair sharing [S3]. There is **no ELG for shape, space and measure**; the maths educational programme instead says the curriculum should give rich chances to build spatial reasoning "including shape, space and measures" [S3]. The Communication & Language ELGs require using recently introduced vocabulary, explaining why things happen, and using conjunctions; the Natural World ELG requires knowing about the seasons and "changing states of matter"; Creating with Materials names colour, texture, form and function [S3]. The ELG wording read matches what was in the 2021/2024 versions as far as we know (we did not diff the two PDFs line by line).

### F2. Development Matters supplies the pre-school comparison words the ELGs leave out  `evidence: guideline (non-statutory, DfE)`
Development Matters (DfE, last updated Sept 2023) [S4] fills the gap for shape, space and measure. Birth-to-three: compare size and weight with gesture and words such as bigger/little/smaller, high/low, tall, heavy. Ages 3–4: compare quantities with "more than"/"fewer than"; talk about 2D/3D shapes with words like sides, corners, straight, flat, round; understand position from words alone ("under the table" with no pointing); use in front of, behind, in, on, under, up, down, beside, between; compare size, length, weight and capacity; spot, continue and fix ABAB patterns; sequence events with first/then and earlier/later. Reception: rotate and combine shapes, see shapes inside shapes, continue and create repeating patterns, compare length, weight and capacity. Understanding the World suggestions add forces they can feel (stretch, snap, bend, magnets attract/repel), melting, sinking/floating, shining light through some materials but not others, and shadows. Expressive Arts adds colour mixing, pitch matching, singing the "melodic shape" (up and down), and moving to changes such as music suddenly getting louder.

### F3. KS1 maths states the comparison duty in one line; the word list is in the non-statutory notes  `evidence: guideline (statutory + non-statutory)`
National curriculum maths (DfE 2013, updated 2021) [S1]. **Year 1 measurement (statutory):** compare, describe and solve practical problems for length and height, mass/weight, capacity and volume, and time; begin to measure and record them; know coins and notes; order events (before/after, next, first, today, yesterday, tomorrow, morning, afternoon, evening); days, weeks, months, years; tell the time to the hour and half past. **Year 1 non-statutory vocabulary**: long/short, longer/shorter, tall/short, double/half; heavy/light, heavier than, lighter than; full/empty, more than, less than, half, half full, quarter; quicker, slower, earlier, later. The note also says mass and weight, and volume and capacity, can be used interchangeably at this stage. **Year 2 measurement:** choose standard units (m, cm, kg, g, °C, litres, ml) with rulers, scales, thermometers and jugs; compare and order lengths, mass, volume/capacity and record with >, < and =; £ and p, combinations and change; compare and sequence intervals of time; time to five minutes including quarter past/to; minutes in an hour, hours in a day.

### F4. Number, fractions and statistics: the "more/less/same" family runs all through KS1  `evidence: guideline (statutory)`
**Year 1** [S1]: count forwards and backwards to and past 100; one more and one less; represent numbers with objects, pictures and a number line using equal to, more than, less than (fewer), most, least; number bonds within 20; read/write +, −, =; solve one-step ×/÷ problems with objects and arrays; recognise a half (one of two equal parts) and a quarter (one of four equal parts) of an object, shape or quantity. **Year 2**: tens and ones; compare and order 0–100 with <, > and =; addition is commutative but subtraction is not (same for × and ÷); odd and even; 2, 5, 10 tables; find 1/3, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4 of a length, shape, set or quantity and see that 2/4 equals 1/2; **statistics** — pictograms, tally charts, block diagrams and tables, sorting categories by quantity, totalling and comparing. Year 1 has no statistics requirement.

### F5. KS1 position and direction: the full word list is non-statutory but explicit  `evidence: guideline (statutory + non-statutory)`
**Year 1 statutory** [S1]: describe position, direction and movement including whole, half, quarter and three-quarter turns. **Non-statutory list**: left and right; top, middle and bottom; on top of, in front of, above, between, around, near, close and far, up and down, forwards and backwards, inside and outside; link clockwise turning to the clock face. **Year 2**: order and arrange objects in patterns and sequences; describe movement including in a straight line; describe quarter, half and three-quarter turns as right angles, clockwise and anticlockwise. KS1 geography adds simple compass directions (N, S, E, W) and suggests locational/directional words such as near and far, left and right [S15]. The Model Music Curriculum (Year 2) also asks children to know left from right to walk in time with others [S9].

### F6. KS1 geometry: name, describe, and recognise shapes in any orientation  `evidence: guideline (statutory + DfE non-statutory guidance)`
Year 1: recognise and name common 2D shapes (rectangles, squares, circles, triangles) and 3D shapes (cuboids, cubes, pyramids, spheres). Year 2: count sides and see vertical line symmetry in 2D shapes; edges, vertices and faces of 3D shapes; spot 2D shapes on 3D surfaces; compare and sort shapes and everyday objects [S1]. The DfE ready-to-progress criteria add that a Year 1 child must know a shape is still a rectangle/triangle/cuboid/pyramid when it is turned, stretched or a different size ("long and thin" is offered as informal describing language) and must build shapes from smaller shapes (1G-1, 1G-2); Year 2 expects precise property language and comparing shapes by similarities and differences (2G-1) [S11][S12].

### F7. NCETM/DfE ready-to-progress criteria cover number and shape only, not measurement  `evidence: guideline (DfE non-statutory, written with NCETM)`
The DfE *Mathematics guidance: key stages 1 and 2* (July 2020, updated 2021) [S11][S12] sets the core a child must secure before moving up. Year 1: count within 100 forwards and backwards (1NPV-1); reason about where numbers to 20 sit on a number line, comparing with <, > and = — larger numbers are "further along the track" (1NPV-2); facts within 10 (1NF-1); count in 2s, 5s, 10s and through odd numbers (1NF-2); compose and partition numbers to 10 including odd and even (1AS-1); read and use +, −, = equations (1AS-2); shapes in any orientation (1G-1); compose shapes (1G-2). Year 2: tens/ones and non-standard partitioning (2NPV-1); place any two-digit number on the line and name the previous/next multiple of 10 (2NPV-2); fluency within 10 (2NF-1); across 10 (2AS-1); subtraction as **difference**, answering "how many more…?" (2AS-2); within 100 (2AS-3, 2AS-4); repeated addition/multiplication (2MD-1); grouping and division (2MD-2); precise shape language (2G-1). Measurement, time, money and statistics have **no** criteria — they stay curriculum duties but are not the DfE's "must-secure" core. For the app this means comparison of *numbers* (more/fewer, greater/less, difference) is the highest-stakes maths concept family.

### F8. NCETM Mastering Number targets number sense in Reception–Year 2 via short daily sessions  `evidence: practitioner programme (no published outcome data on page)`
NCETM's Maths Hubs project aims for firm number sense from Reception through Year 2 so that children leave KS1 fluent and flexible with number [S13]. Schools run it as a short extra daily session (10–15 minutes per NCETM's earlier project description [S14]); it uses manipulatives such as the rekenrek [S14]. The NCETM page reports teacher case studies only; an EEF/NFER effectiveness trial of the Reception version is running in 2025–26 [S16]. Takeaway for the app: short, frequent, number-structure tasks (subitising, "how many more?", doubles, odd/even) match what Year 2 classrooms already do daily.

### F9. KS1 science names the material-property pairs; light and dark wait until Year 3  `evidence: guideline (statutory + non-statutory)`
**Year 1** [S5]: tell an object from the material it is made of; name wood, plastic, glass, metal, water, rock; describe and compare simple physical properties. Non-statutory property words: hard/soft, stretchy/stiff, shiny/dull, rough/smooth, bendy/not bendy, waterproof/not waterproof, absorbent/not absorbent, opaque/transparent. Also: name common plants including deciduous and evergreen trees; name fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals; carnivores, herbivores, omnivores; body parts and senses; observe the four seasons, their weather and how day length changes. **Year 2**: living, dead, never alive; habitats and simple food chains; seeds/bulbs grow into plants and plants need water, light and warmth; animals grow into adults and need water, food and air; suitability of materials for uses; solids change shape when squashed, bent, twisted, stretched. **Year 3 (not KS1)**: we need light to see; "dark is the absence of light"; reflection; shadows when an opaque object blocks light; shadow size changes [S5]. EYFS guidance already touches shadows and light through materials [S4], so a light/dark mini-game is reasonable enrichment for a Year 2 child but is ahead of the KS1 statute.

### F10. KS1 English supplies the grammar of comparison: -er/-est and conjunctions  `evidence: guideline (statutory)`
**Year 1** [S6][S7]: add -ing, -ed, -er and -est where the root does not change (quicker, quickest); read words with -er/-est endings; the un- prefix; plural -s/-es; join ideas with "and". Appendix 1 adds that adjectives ending in silent e drop it (nicer, nicest). **Year 2**: subordinating (when, if, that, because) and co-ordinating (or, and, but) conjunctions; statements, questions, exclamations and commands; expanded noun phrases ("the blue butterfly"). **Prepositions are not named until Years 3–4** (to express time and cause) — in KS1 the position words live in maths, not English [S6]. Spoken language (all years) asks children to build vocabulary, ask questions and give well-structured descriptions. So "bigger / biggest" is both a maths and an English objective in Year 1.

### F11. KS1 music: pitch, duration, dynamics, tempo are the core pairs, made concrete by the Model Music Curriculum  `evidence: guideline (statutory + DfE non-statutory)`
The statutory KS1 music programme says pupils should explore and combine sounds using the "inter-related dimensions of music", which the aims list as pitch, duration, dynamics, tempo, timbre, texture, structure and notation [S8]. The DfE Model Music Curriculum (March 2021) [S9] makes it concrete. **Year 1**: walk/clap a steady beat that changes speed with the music; copy rhythms; compare high and low sounds around school; sing in high and low voices; ascending xylophone notes for climbing, quiet shakers for rain. **Year 2**: know dynamics (loud/quiet) and tempo (fast/slow) and respond to symbols for getting louder/quieter and pause; the beat can speed up or slow down; group beats in 2s and 3s (strong first beat); stick notation with crotchets, quavers and crotchet rests; so-mi singing games; show pitch changes with actions (stand up/sit down, hands high/low); match dot notation to 3-note tunes. The KS2 element table then names high/low and rising/falling, fast/slow, loud/quiet, getting faster/louder.

### F12. KS1 art lists seven visual elements; "tone" (light/dark shades) is not one of them  `evidence: guideline (statutory)`
KS1 art and design requires drawing, painting and sculpture and developing techniques in "colour, pattern, texture, line, shape, form and space" [S10]. Tone is not listed; Development Matters mentions tones only in passing for babies' sensory materials, and colour mixing for 3–4s [S4]. Lighter/darker colour is therefore an enrichment concept for Year 2 (commonly taught in KS2 schemes), though "colour" itself is statutory and colour mixing is expected in EYFS.

### F13. The curriculum will change in September 2028; KS1 number will be resequenced  `evidence: government policy paper`
The government accepted most of the Curriculum and Assessment Review (Nov 2025): a revised national curriculum is due by spring 2027 for first teaching from September 2028, and maths KS1–3 will be reviewed "with a particular focus on resequencing content" in the number domain [S17]. The current 2013/2014 programmes remain the ones in force for a child in Year 2 in 2026–27. The concept pairs below are unlikely to vanish, but year placements may shift; re-check in 2027.

## Design rules for the app
- R1. **Use the curriculum's own words** as the spoken prompts and answer labels: longer/shorter, heavier than/lighter than, full/empty/half full, quicker/slower, earlier/later, on top of/in front of/between. A teacher or parent will recognise them, and they are the words he is assessed with (F3, F5, F9).
- R2. **Order of introduction inside each concept**: two-item comparison (bigger) → three items with -est (biggest) → put 3–4 in order → symbols <, >, = (Year 2). This mirrors EYFS → Y1 → Y2 (F1, F3, F10).
- R3. **Units (11), first to last**, each 6–15 pairs from the table:
  1. *Size & amount* (#1–5, 8–10) — EYFS-secure warm-up, fast wins.
  2. *Piano sounds* (#107–116) — high/low, loud/quiet, fast/slow, long/short: the natural pop-up inside piano play (F11).
  3. *Where is it?* position (#57–68) (F5).
  4. *Measure it* length/height (#28–35), weight (#36–38), capacity (#39–41) (F3).
  5. *Number compare* (#6–7, 11–22) — highest priority maths family per ready-to-progress (F7, F8).
  6. *Time & speed* (#44–54).
  7. *Shapes & patterns* (#73–82, 69–71) (F6).
  8. *Fractions & sharing* (#13, 23–27).
  9. *What is it made of?* materials (#83–97) (F9).
  10. *Nature & seasons* (#98–103, 42–43, 106).
  11. *Light, colour & enrichment* (#104–105, 117–121, 55–56, 72, 127) — Year 3 / non-KS1 items last and flagged as stretch (F9, F12).
  Language items (#122–126) are not a unit: they are woven into answers ("Yes — the elephant is **heavier**").
- R4. **Disambiguate words with two meanings** before mixing units: *light* (not heavy) vs *light* (pale colour) vs *light* (not dark); *short* (not long) vs *short* (not tall); *high* (up on a shelf) vs *high* (pitch); *soft* (material) vs *soft* (quiet). Keep each meaning in its own unit with a context icon (scale, paint pot, torch, music note) (F3, F9, F11, F12).
- R5. **On the piano, map pitch carefully**: on a keyboard "higher" notes are to the right, not physically up. Show high/low pitch with a vertical animation (bird up / bear down) *and* the keyboard, and say "higher notes are on the right side of the piano" (F11, F5).
- R6. **Maths "compare" pop-ups get the most frequent rotation** (number more/fewer, difference "how many more?", doubles, odd/even), because they are the DfE must-secure core for Year 1–2 and are what Mastering Number drills daily (F7, F8).
- R7. **Show shapes in many orientations and proportions** (thin triangles, upside-down pyramids, squares on a point) so the child does not learn "triangle = pointing up" (F6).
- R8. **Treat weight/mass and capacity/volume as the same word for now**; speak "heavier" and "holds more", not "mass" or "volume" (F3).
- R9. **Never let colour alone carry the answer** (hot/cold, darker/lighter, red/blue): add an icon, a sound or a size change (links to file 05).
- R10. **Tag every question with its table row number and curriculum year** in the app data, so a parent screen can show "Year 1 maths: position — 9/12 secure" and so the bank can be re-mapped when the 2028 curriculum moves things (F13).

## Open questions / conflicting evidence
- The EYFS 2026 framework replaced the 2024 one on 1 Sept 2026; the ELG wording we read appears unchanged from earlier versions, but we did not do a full line-by-line diff.
- The DfE ready-to-progress guidance (with NCETM) covers no measurement, time, money or statistics, so there is no official "must-secure" ranking for those; our unit order for them is a judgement.
- "Lighter/darker" colour (tone) and "light/dark" (vision) are not KS1 statutory; the app's planned darker/lighter game is enrichment. Whether tone is better taught at 6 is not settled by these sources.
- Prepositions are named in English only from Years 3–4; KS1 position words sit in maths. Whether to teach them as "maths" or "language" in the app is a presentation choice.
- Mastering Number session length (10–15 min) and the rekenrek come from NCETM project descriptions; the current NCETM page did not restate them, and outcome data (EEF trial) are not yet published.
- New national curriculum from Sept 2028: KS1 number content may be resequenced; Year placements in the table may need updating in 2027.
- Several table rows (#35, #80, #121) are reasonable inferences from guidance examples rather than named requirements; they are marked "informal" or "inferred".
