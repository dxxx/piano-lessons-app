# Rivals teach adults; your app can teach gently

**Bottom line:** Your app already has the mechanics the best paid apps charge for: wait mode, hands separate with the other hand played softly (Synthesia's "Background" part), slow speeds, a listen-first preview and 1-3 stars. Its real advantage is the on-screen piano, because the press is known exactly. The paid apps' top complaint is missed-note detection by microphone, and you have none. No product I found is built for a 6-year-old. Skoove's own help centre says "kids aged 7 and up" ([Skoove help, 30 Jul 2025](https://help.skoove.com/en/articles/5417621-who-can-use-skoove-can-children-learn-with-skoove)), and Simply's child mode covers only ages 4-10 in its first few courses ([Simply help, 15 Jun 2026](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/8053114-is-the-app-experience-different-for-children)). The evidence for almost every mechanic is thin: there is no controlled study of piano-app features in 5-7-year-olds, and most guidance is teacher opinion or borrowed from adult and motor-learning research. So the best additions are cheap, reversible and low-pressure rather than copies of rival gamification. The top four, in order: a calm wrong-note response (hint the right key, never buzz), a "keep going" step after clean wait-mode passes, phrase-sized looping, and fading finger numbers or hand icons. Avoid streaks, leaderboards, countdowns and red failure states, which the best-sourced child-app research links to manipulative design. A free app with no ads and no streak penalties is itself a point of difference.

A note on sources. The researchers worked from help centres, store listings, reviews and abstracts, not from full papers or hands-on testing. I keep their labels: "unverified", "abstract only", "opinion", "PRE-2024". Where two notes disagree, I show both figures.

## How the paid apps teach a beginner

The apps split into three families. **Song-first, microphone-driven apps** (Simply Piano, flowkey, Skoove) listen to an acoustic piano and colour or pause the screen. **Sheet-music and MIDI-driven apps** (Piano Marvel, Playground Sessions, Melodics) need a MIDI keyboard and score accuracy and timing. **Falling-note players** (Synthesia, Perfect Piano, Simpia) show blocks dropping onto a keyboard. Yousician sits across these, offering four views.

Cueing differs mostly in how much notation is shown. flowkey splits the screen between an overhead video of a pianist's hands and scrolling sheet music, with upcoming notes lit on a keyboard graphic ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1020357408)). Skoove shows sheet music from lesson 1 and runs Listen, Learn, Play stages (secondary summary; the page itself was not fetched). Synthesia is the fullest reference for your screen: left hand usually blue and right hand green, optional finger numbers on blocks, and in Melody Practice coloured dots on the keys with the app holding until the correct note ([Synthesia guide](https://synthesia.app/support/guide/firstSong)). Its "Parts" system lets each track be Left, Right, Background (the app plays it) or Custom ([Synthesia song setup](https://synthesia.app/support/guide/songSetup)). Yousician offers enhanced, falling-notes, coloured and sheet views, with finger colours (thumb green through little finger red) and numbers 1-5 on falling notes ([Yousician help](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/208678605-Notation-options-in-piano); the article may be 2024-era). For children, Simply swaps finger numbers for hand icons in its first courses ([Simply help](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/8053114-is-the-app-experience-different-for-children)).

Wrong-note handling is the least documented area. Only Simply documents it officially: blue is correct, yellow is correct after a hint, and red appears only in "moving stages"; if you play wrongly the correct key is highlighted yellow ([Simply help, 14 Sep 2026](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/7943490-learning-with-simply-piano-the-basics)). flowkey's Wait Mode "waits for you to hit the right notes and chords", while "Just Play" keeps up "even if you miss a few notes" ([Google Play listing, 16 Oct 2026](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.flowkey.app&hl=en&gl=GB)). Nobody documents whether a chord needs all keys or any key. Playground Sessions turns notes green or red, and a Trustpilot reviewer says its grading demands near-perfect notes and timing ([Trustpilot, 2.5/5 from 127 reviews](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/playgroundsessions.com)). Melodics uses timing colours and resets a streak on a miss ([Melodics scoring](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15092017-lesson-scoring)). One teacher review lists flowkey's feedback as "too forgiving" (opinion). The pattern: the beginner-friendly apps pause or hint, and the demanding ones score strictly.

Practice tools show the same split. flowkey has Loop, Select a Hand, Wait Mode, and 50% and 75% speeds, but no built-in metronome ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/best-piano-learning-apps-in-2026-an-in-depth-comparison-of-music-education-technology), reviewer). Yousician is the only app with a documented loop mechanism (sliders on the progress bar plus an optional one-bar count-in), and its auto-adjust speed runs from 25% to 125% in 5% steps ([Yousician help](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/204796991-Practice-and-Play-modes-in-piano)). Skoove has a Tempo button, though not in every step ([Skoove help, PRE-2024](https://help.skoove.com/en/articles/5421914-can-i-adjust-the-tempo-of-the-pieces-on-skoove)). Simply's tempo options appear only in "moving stages", its Play and Feedback features are iOS only, and no loop is listed ([Simply help](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/7943680-understanding-play)). Synthesia has wait, hands, speed, loops, bookmarks and a metronome ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/synthesia/id579518445)). Your speed button, wait mode and hand split match the leaders. The missing pieces are looping and finger guidance.

Progression and rewards are where the apps diverge most from a child's needs. flowkey sells every song at four levels and Simply at three (Essentials, Intermediate, Pre-Advanced). Yousician gives one silver to three gold stars from accuracy and timing, with a 3x-5x points multiplier. Simply gives up to 3 stars per song, weekly streaks with "freezes", and 5-minute workouts ([Simply streaks, 15 Jun 2026](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/15496066-how-do-streaks-work-in-simply-piano)). Its iOS-only kids profiles add XP, levels and unlockable rewards such as Tempo Rush, which plays a song at up to 200% ([Simply help, 12 Jul 2026](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/15913867-how-do-xp-and-rewards-work-kids-profiles)). Piano Marvel's SASR sight-reading test scores 100-1900 adaptively and is adult and teacher-oriented ([Piano Marvel](https://pianomarvel.com/feature/sasr)). The notes do not say how flowkey or Simply simplify each level, so "derive easier versions from MIDI" is your own design, not a copied method.

## What the evidence says for a 6-year-old

Ratings below are the researchers' own; I have not upgraded any. The notes found **no child-participant study** for any piano-app mechanic.

| Wait mode | WEAK-MODERATE (adjacent only) | Duke, Simmons and Cash (2009): 17 advanced adult pianists, one 3-bar passage. Two themes run through the table. The strongest findings concern **practice quality** and **motivation design**, not individual features. And sound-before-symbol teaching (Kodály, Orff, Suzuki, Gordon) is the tradition behind Hoffman's singing-first start ([Pianist's Compass, 28 Jan 2026](https://pianistscompass.org/?p=12549)); it supports ear and beat work before notation, but the notes give it only as a tradition summary, not a controlled test. Method-book order is similar across publishers: keyboard orientation by black-key groups, middle C position with finger numbers, five-finger patterns, steps and skips, note values, then staff from landmark notes. Faber Primer is documented, but the researchers could not confirm when hands-together or finger-number fading begin, and the standard RH 1-5 / LH 5-1 layout on C-G was not sourced; verify before shipping.

## Ideas for our app

**Already in the app (do not propose again):** exact-press on-screen piano, MIDI songs, wait-mode "Follow the keys" with pace adaptation, right/left/both hands with the other hand soft, Auto/50/75/100% speed, 20-second listen preview, favourites, 1-3 stars, free-play "Beat" metronome, spoken guidance (TTS), PIN-locked grown-ups menu. ## Do not copy

Streak counters with "freezes" (Simply), points multipliers (Yousician 3x-5x), XP and level-up unlocks (Simply kids profile), leaderboards, Melodics-style streak reset on a single miss, and Playground-style near-perfect grading all sit close to the "lures" Radesky coded and the undermining effects Deci et al. report (contested). Do not add countdowns or timers; Radesky coded time pressure in 17.3% of apps. Do not use red for wrong notes; Simply confines it to moving stages, and a gentle hint does the same job. Do not copy Tempo Rush-style speed-ups (Simply, up to 200%) as a reward; nothing suggests speed matters to a 6-year-old at this stage. Do not add a microphone path: missed notes are the paid apps' dominant complaint, and you have none. Do not build Piano Marvel-style adaptive sight-reading; it rests on calibrated data and a sight-reading team ([Piano Marvel](https://pianomarvel.com/feature/sasr)). Do not copy paywalls, auto-renewal and family-plan pricing; Simply has the heaviest billing complaints of the apps covered (Unstar 2026, an aggregator). Finally, ignore online claims such as "30% less likely to read sheet music": the researchers judged those pages generated and rejected them.

## Conclusion

The notes confirm a gap: the nearest precedents for a child-first, on-screen design are Simply's Touch Courses and Casio's lit keys, and none pairs exact key detection with a no-fail tone for a 6-year-old. The sharper insight is restraint.
